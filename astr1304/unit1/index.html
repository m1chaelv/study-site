Chapter 1 — Charting the Heavens

Science seeks natural explanations for observed phenomena. The three hallmarks of science are:

  • Seeks explanations based on natural causes only (not supernatural)
  • Progresses through creation and testing of models that explain observations as simply as possible
  • Makes testable predictions — if predictions fail, the model must be revised or abandoned
Science gives us predictive power — e.g., understanding lightning as an electrical phenomenon lets us protect against it. No supernatural explanation can do that.
  • Observation — notice a natural phenomenon
  • Hypothesis — educated guess for the cause
  • Experiment/Test — gather data to test the hypothesis
  • Conclusion — does data support or contradict?
  • If contradicted → revise hypothesis and repeat
  • If supported → independent reproduction by others required
  • After repeated confirmation → hypothesis promoted to Theory
A scientific theory is the END product of the method — not the beginning. It represents our current best understanding and can still be revised with new evidence.

Evidence is the deciding factor in science. A hypothesis is never accepted based on the status of the person proposing it — only on reproducible experimental evidence. Scientific theories require an overwhelming amount of evidence to support them (e.g., Darwin's theory of evolution).

"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence." — Carl Sagan
  • Hypothesis — an educated guess that has not yet been rigorously tested
  • Scientific Theory — a hypothesis (or group of hypotheses) supported by repeated testing and independent reproduction

In everyday language "it's just a theory" implies uncertainty. In science, a theory is our most reliable explanation. Valid theories must be: testable, reproducible, simple, and make testable predictions. A statement like "the universe was pink before the Big Bang" is NOT a scientific theory — it cannot be tested.

  • Observation: Uranus deviated from its predicted orbital path
  • Framework: Newton's Law of Gravity
  • Hypothesis: An undiscovered outer planet perturbs Uranus' orbit via gravity
  • Prediction: Le Verrier & Adams independently calculated where this planet should be
  • Experiment: September 23, 1846 — Neptune found within 1° of Le Verrier's prediction

The celestial sphere is an ancient conceptual model — an imaginary sphere surrounding Earth on which stars appear fixed. It is NOT real, just a useful tool for understanding sky motion.

The word "planet" comes from the Greek asters planetei ("wandering stars") — five objects visible to the naked eye that moved across the sky relative to the fixed stars: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn.

Constellations are 2D projections of stars at vastly different distances — they are NOT real physical clusters. Stars in a constellation are typically far apart in 3D space. Ancient cultures used them to track seasons. Astrology and horoscopes are not science.

Seasons are caused by Earth's axial tilt of ~23.5° relative to the plane of its orbit (ecliptic).

⚠️ Common misconception: Seasons are NOT caused by Earth's distance to the Sun changing — they are caused by the changing angle of illumination.
  • Summer Solstice: North Pole tilted toward Sun → longest day
  • Winter Solstice: North Pole tilted away → shortest day
  • Equinox: Both poles equidistant from Sun → equal day and night
  • Precession: Earth's axis wobbles with a 26,000-year cycle due to gravitational pull of Moon and Sun

Lunar phases are caused by the Moon's position relative to Earth and the Sun as it orbits Earth every 29.5 days. The Moon's brightness comes from reflected sunlight.

⚠️ Lunar phases are NOT caused by Earth's shadow on the Moon. That's a lunar eclipse — a different phenomenon.
  • Moon is tidally locked — we always see the same side
  • New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Third Quarter → Waning Crescent
  • Solar Eclipse: Moon passes directly in front of the Sun — only possible during New Moon
  • Lunar Eclipse: Earth's shadow falls on the Moon — only possible during Full Moon
  • Eclipses don't happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted ~5.2° from the ecliptic — alignment only occurs during "eclipse seasons"

Chapter 2 — The Copernican Revolution

  • Prehistoric: Lascaux cave paintings (~15,000 yr ago) may be earliest lunar calendar; Great Pyramid aligned to true north (2560 BC); Stonehenge (2800–1400 BC) as a calendar
  • Greek Astronomy (600 BC–400 AD): Greatest early contributions — Aristarchus proposed heliocentric model 2000 years before Copernicus; Eratosthenes measured Earth's circumference; Hipparcos catalogued 850 stars; Ptolemy codified geocentric model in the Almagest
  • Islamic Scholars (Dark Ages): Azophi discovered Andromeda Galaxy (964 AD); Ibn al-Haytham established experimental confirmation in science; Arabic star names (e.g., Aldebaran) still used today
  • Geocentric (Ptolemy): Earth at center; planets move in circles + epicycles to explain retrograde motion; included in the Almagest
  • Heliocentric (Copernicus, 1473–1543): Sun at center; Earth is one of 6 known planets; simpler model; derived that planets closer to the Sun move faster
  • Retrograde motion is naturally explained in the heliocentric model — Earth overtakes outer planets, making them appear to move backward temporarily

Galileo (1564–1642) was the first to use a telescope astronomically (not its inventor). His key observations that supported heliocentrism:

  • Sunspots → the Sun is not perfect and it rotates (~1 month)
  • Craters on the Moon → heavenly bodies are not perfect spheres
  • Phases of Venus → Venus shows full phases like the Moon, impossible in the geocentric model where Venus always stays between Earth and Sun
  • Moons of Jupiter → not everything orbits Earth; destroyed the geocentric assumption

Tycho Brahe (1546–1601): meticulous observational astronomer who made the most precise naked-eye measurements of planetary positions ever recorded. He designed his own instruments. Ironically, he still favored a geocentric model. His data were used by Kepler to derive the 3 laws of planetary motion.

Johannes Kepler (1571–1630) used Brahe's data to derive three empirical laws:

  • 1st Law (Ellipses): Planetary orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus (not the center). Described by eccentricity e; e=0 is a circle.
  • 2nd Law (Equal Areas): A line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times → planets move faster when closer to the Sun (perihelion) and slower when farther (aphelion). Explained by conservation of angular momentum.
  • 3rd Law (P²=a³): The square of the orbital period (years) equals the cube of the semi-major axis (AU): P² = a³. Closer planets orbit faster.
  • 1st Law (Inertia): Objects at rest stay at rest; objects in motion stay in motion unless acted upon by a force
  • 2nd Law (F=ma): Force = mass × acceleration
  • 3rd Law (Action-Reaction): Every action has an equal and opposite reaction
  • Law of Gravity: F = G·M₁·M₂ / r² — gravity decreases with the square of the distance; explains Kepler's laws
  • Weight vs. Mass: Mass (kg) is the amount of matter — constant anywhere. Weight is the gravitational force on that mass — varies by location.
  • Escape velocity (Earth): 11.2 km/s to leave Earth's gravity entirely. Orbital velocity: ~7.8 km/s for low Earth orbit.
  • Astronauts are weightless because they are in free fall, not because there is no gravity

Chapter 6 — The Solar System: Formation & Comparative Planetology

  • All planets orbit in roughly the same plane (few degrees of each other) — solar system is very flat
  • All planets orbit in the same direction as the Sun spins
  • Most orbits are nearly circular (low eccentricity)
  • Inner solar system (within ~1.5 AU): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, then the Asteroid Belt
  • Outer solar system: Jupiter (5.2 AU), Saturn (~10 AU), Uranus (20 AU), Neptune (30 AU)
  • 8 major planets (Pluto is NOT one — it's a Kuiper Belt Object)
  • Asteroid Belt: Between Mars and Jupiter; rocky/metallic bodies; NOT dangerously dense like Star Wars depicts
  • Kuiper Belt: Beyond Neptune's orbit; icy objects including Pluto; predicted by Gerard Kuiper; visited by New Horizons spacecraft ~2015
  • Comets: Ice-rich bodies on highly elliptical orbits; develop tails when heating causes subsurface ice to sublimate near the Sun; tails can span astronomical units
  • Terrestrial planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars): small, high density, rocky/metallic composition, solid surfaces, few/no moons, no rings
  • Gas Giants (Jupiter, Saturn): massive, low density, primarily H/He gas, no solid surface — a spacecraft cannot fly through; pressure increases to ~100 million times Earth's surface pressure at Jupiter's core
  • Ice Giants (Uranus, Neptune): smaller than gas giants but still larger than Earth; ~80% of mass is water/ammonia/methane ices + rocky core; only ~20% H/He gas — fundamentally distinct from gas giants despite looking similar from outside
⚠️ Common trap: Ice giants are NOT mini gas giants. Their bulk composition is dominated by ices, not H/He gas.

Age of solar system: 4.56 billion years. Formation process:

  • A giant interstellar gas/dust cloud collapsed under its own gravity (triggered by nearby supernova shockwave)
  • Conservation of angular momentum caused the collapsing cloud to spin faster and flatten into a protoplanetary disc around the young Sun
  • We observe protoplanetary discs around other young stars today (e.g., Hubble images of the Orion Nebula)

Gaseous material in the protoplanetary disc condensed into solid particles based on temperature. Closer to the Sun (hotter): only high-melting-point metals and rocks can condense → rocky/metallic terrestrial planets. Further from the Sun (cooler, beyond the "frost line"): ices (water, ammonia, methane) also condense → raw material for much larger ice/gas giant cores.

The condensation sequence naturally explains WHY rocky planets are in the inner solar system and gas/ice giants in the outer solar system.

Planetesimals are the "Lego building blocks" of planets — small solid bodies formed by condensation. Accretion is the process by which gravity pulls these together into progressively larger bodies (protoplanets → planets). Gas giants additionally accreted huge H/He gas envelopes once their solid cores reached ~10 Earth masses, triggering runaway gas accretion.

  • Gas giants: Had to form in <5 million years before the young Sun's solar wind expelled all the disc gas
  • Terrestrial planets: Could take up to ~100 million years (don't depend on gas)
  • Asteroids, comets, and Kuiper Belt objects are leftover planetesimals from regions where no major planet fully formed
  • Jupiter's gravity prevented planet formation in the asteroid belt region
CH 1 Charting the Heavens
The Scientific Method — Flow

Observation → Hypothesis → Experiment → Conclusion → (if contradicted) Revise hypothesis → (if supported) Independent reproduction → Theory. A theory is the end product, not the beginning. It must be testable, reproducible, and simple.

Hypothesis vs. Theory

A hypothesis is an educated guess. A scientific theory has survived extensive testing and independent reproduction. Popular misuse of "theory" as "just a guess" is wrong — in science a theory is our strongest explanation.

Example: "The universe was pink before the Big Bang" is NOT a theory — it cannot be tested or falsified.
Neptune — Scientific Method in Action
  • Uranus's orbit showed unexplained deviations
  • Hypothesis: an undiscovered planet's gravity perturbs Uranus
  • Le Verrier & Adams independently predicted position
  • Confirmed Sep 23, 1846 — within 1° of Le Verrier's prediction
Celestial Sphere

An imaginary sphere surrounding Earth on which stars appear to be fixed. NOT real — just a conceptual model. The apparent rotation is caused by Earth's actual rotation. Planets move relative to the fixed stars because they orbit the Sun at different distances (closer = faster apparent motion).

Constellations

2D projections of stars at very different distances — they do not form real clusters. Used historically to track seasons and navigation. Astrology/horoscopes are not science.

Seasons — Cause

Earth's axial tilt of ~23.5° changes the angle at which sunlight strikes the surface throughout the year. Higher angle = more intense heat concentrated on less area. NOT caused by changing distance from Sun.

At solstices, one pole points toward/away from the Sun. At equinoxes, both poles are equidistant — day = night globally.
Lunar Phases

Caused by the Moon's position relative to Earth and Sun as it orbits in 29.5 days. Phases are NOT caused by Earth's shadow. Moon is tidally locked — same face always visible.

Sequence: New → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full → Waning Gibbous → Third Quarter → Waning Crescent → New

Eclipses
  • Solar Eclipse: Moon blocks Sun from Earth's view. Only at New Moon. Requires Moon to be at a node (intersection of Moon's orbital plane with ecliptic).
  • Lunar Eclipse: Earth's shadow falls on Full Moon. Only at Full Moon.
  • Eclipses don't happen monthly because Moon's orbit is tilted ~5.2° from ecliptic — alignment is rare.
CH 2 The Copernican Revolution
Key Historical Figures
  • Ptolemy: Geocentric model with epicycles, codified in the Almagest
  • Copernicus (1473–1543): Sun-centered model; planets orbit in order of distance
  • Tycho Brahe (1546–1601): Most precise naked-eye observations ever; still geocentric believer; data inherited by Kepler
  • Galileo (1564–1642): Telescope observations proved heliocentrism — phases of Venus, Jupiter's moons, sunspots, Moon craters
  • Kepler (1571–1630): Used Brahe's data to derive 3 laws of planetary motion
  • Newton (1642–1727): Explained WHY Kepler's laws work through gravity and motion laws
Kepler's Laws
  • 1st: Orbits are ellipses, Sun at one focus. Semi-major axis = a (AU). Eccentricity e: 0 = circle.
  • 2nd: Equal areas swept in equal times → faster at perihelion, slower at aphelion. Due to conservation of angular momentum.
  • 3rd: P² = a³ — (P in years, a in AU). Confirmed for all 8 planets.
Newton's Laws of Motion
  • 1st (Inertia): Objects maintain their state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by a force
  • 2nd (F=ma): Force equals mass times acceleration
  • 3rd (Action-Reaction): Equal and opposite forces
Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

F = G·M₁·M₂ / r²

Gravity is always attractive, depends on both masses, weakens with the square of the distance, and is universal (same on Earth and in space). This explains all of Kepler's laws and planetary orbits.

Weight vs. Mass; Orbits & Escape
  • Mass (kg): intrinsic amount of matter — constant everywhere
  • Weight (N or lb): gravitational force on that mass — varies by location
  • Orbital velocity (low Earth): ~7.8 km/s — object "falls" but keeps overshooting Earth's curved surface
  • Escape velocity (Earth): 11.2 km/s
  • Astronauts feel weightless because they are in free fall, not because gravity is absent
  • Newton revised Kepler's 3rd law: (M₁+M₂)P² = a³
CH 6 Solar System: Comparative Planetology & Formation
Orbital Configuration
  • All planets orbit in the same plane (ecliptic) & same direction as the Sun's rotation
  • Nearly circular orbits (low eccentricity) — Mercury has the highest at ~0.206
  • Inner solar system is compact (<1.5 AU); outer solar system spans 5–30 AU
Comparative Planetology
  • Terrestrial: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars — rocky, high density, small
  • Gas Giants: Jupiter, Saturn — mostly H/He gas; no solid surface to land on — a spacecraft would be crushed by increasing pressure long before reaching a core; pressure at Jupiter's center is ~100 million times Earth's surface pressure
  • Ice Giants: Uranus, Neptune — ~80% ices (water, ammonia, methane) + rocky core; only ~20% H/He gas; distinct from gas giants despite looking similar from the outside
Non-Planetary Components
  • Asteroids: Rocky/metallic; mostly in the Asteroid Belt (Mars–Jupiter); NOT dangerously dense
  • Kuiper Belt Objects (KBOs): Icy bodies beyond Neptune's orbit; Pluto is a KBO (not a planet) because thousands of similar objects exist
  • Comets: Ice-rich, highly elliptical orbits; develop tails when solar heat sublimes subsurface ice; two types: short-period (from Kuiper Belt) and long-period (from Oort Cloud)
Solar System Formation — Nebular Hypothesis
  • Solar system age: 4.56 billion years
  • Giant interstellar cloud collapsed under gravity → conservation of angular momentum → spun faster → flattened into a protoplanetary disc
  • Protoplanetary discs observed around other young stars today
  • Model must explain: planets in same plane, same orbital direction, nearly circular orbits, differentiation (rocky inner vs. gas/ice outer), and the non-planetary components
Orbital Properties — Which Are Evidence of Disk Formation?

A successful formation model must explain several properties. Know which ones directly result from forming in a disc and which do not:

  • ✅ All planets orbit in nearly the same plane → direct result of disc formation
  • ✅ All planets orbit in the same direction as the Sun rotates → result of disc formation
  • ✅ Orbits nearly circular → result of formation in a disc
  • ✅ Planets orbit near the Sun's equatorial plane → result of disc formation
  • ❌ Many planets have moons → NOT direct evidence of disc formation; moons form by separate processes (capture, giant impacts, sub-discs around planets)
⚠️ Exam trap: "Having moons" is the one property that is NOT a direct indicator that planets formed in a disc.
Condensation Sequence

Temperature in the protoplanetary disc decreased with distance from the young Sun. Different materials condense (go from gas to solid) at different temperatures — this is the condensation sequence:

  • Inner disc (hot): Metals and rocks condense first (high melting points) → these are the building blocks of terrestrial planets. Both metals AND rocks condense here — not just metals.
  • Outer disc (cool, beyond frost/snow line): Ices (water, ammonia, methane) also condense → vastly more solid material → much larger cores → gas/ice giants
⚠️ Exam trap: The condensation sequence does NOT mean only metals condensed in the inner disc — rocks condensed there too. Ices condensed ONLY in the outer disc, not the inner disc.
Planetesimal Accretion & Ultima Thule

Accretion = the gradual growth of larger objects by gravitationally attracting smaller objects. Condensed particles clump → planetesimals (roughly km-sized, massive enough to gravitationally attract others) → protoplanets → planets.

Ultima Thule (Arrokoth) is a Kuiper Belt Object visited by New Horizons in 2019. It clearly consists of two smaller objects that gently stuck together — a contact binary — making it a beautiful direct demonstration of planetesimal accretion in action.

Accretion ≠ condensation. Condensation turns gas into tiny solid grains. Accretion then grows those grains into larger and larger bodies through gravity.
Giant Planet Formation: Core Accretion vs. Disk Instability
  • Core Accretion (favored): A solid rock/ice core forms beyond the ice line via planetesimal accretion. Once the core reaches ~10 Earth masses, its gravity rapidly pulls in H/He gas from the disc (runaway gas accretion) → gas giant. Supported by Saturn's detected dense core and exoplanet observations.
  • Disk Instability (less favored): Dense regions of the disc collapse directly under gravity — analogous to how a star forms from an interstellar gas cloud, but happening within the protoplanetary disc. Does not require a solid core first. Requires an unusually massive disc.
⚠️ Key distinction: Core accretion needs a rock/ice core FIRST, then accretes gas. Disk instability directly collapses gas — like star formation. The fill-in-the-blank answer for "disk instability forms a planet like a ___" is star.
Formation Timescales
  • Gas Giants: Must form in <5 million years — because the young Sun's intense radiation and stellar wind (T-Tauri phase) physically expels all remaining H/He gas from the disc after a few million years. Once the gas is gone, no more gas giants can form.
  • Terrestrial Planets: Up to ~100 million years — they don't depend on disc gas, just solid material, so they have much more time
  • ⚠️ Counterintuitive fact: Gas giants (more massive) formed FASTER than terrestrial planets (less massive). The time limit is set by disc gas dispersal, not by planet mass.
  • Asteroids, KBOs, and comets = leftover planetesimals — regions where no major planet fully formed due to insufficient material density or Jupiter's gravitational disruption
