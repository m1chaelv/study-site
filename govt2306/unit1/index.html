Chapter 1 — The Environment of Texas Politics
Political Culture, Geography, Economy & Policy Challenges
▼
Political Culture — Elazar's Three Types
Political culture = attitudes, habits, and general behavior patterns that develop over time and affect the political life of a state.

Moralistic: New England Puritan roots. Political participation = civic duty. Government = public service for the common good. Least common in Texas.
Individualistic: Shaped by the frontier experience. Limited government, rugged self-reliance, pro-business, anti-regulation. Daniel Elazar identifies Texas as strongly individualistic.
Traditionalistic: Rooted in the Old South plantation/patron system. Government maintains the status quo for elite citizens. Historically excluded poor and minorities. Features: right to work, eminent domain, favorable business climate.

Elazar: TX = Individualistic moralistic individualistic traditionalistic frontier experience patron system
Geography — 4 Regions & Key Facts
Texas: 2nd largest state (267,000+ sq mi, behind Alaska) and 2nd in population (29M+, behind California).

1. Texas Great Plains (West Texas) — agriculture, Ogallala Aquifer, Republican
2. Gulf Coastal Plains — Houston is here; petrochemicals
3. Interior Lowlands — Central/North Texas; Austin (govt, universities); Dallas-Fort Worth
4. Basin and Range Province — Trans-Pecos; ONLY mountainous region in Texas

Basin & Range = ONLY mountains Houston = Gulf Coastal Plains 2nd in size and population
Demographics
85% urban today (vs. 80% rural in 1900). Most populous counties: Harris, Dallas, Bexar, Tarrant (combined = 40%+ of state population).
Latinos (~39%) = fastest-growing ethnic group. Anglo population has fallen below 50%.
Policy tools enforcing residential segregation: redlining (discriminatory loan rating), urban renewal (taking property for public projects), gentrification (higher-income displacing lower-income residents).

Latinos = fastest growing 85% urban redlining gentrification
Four Land-Based Industries
1. Cattle — traced from Spain → Mexico → Texas longhorn; Texas still leads the nation
2. Cotton — backbone of economy 1900s; Texas yields ~50% of U.S. supply
3. Timber — Gulf Coastal plains; bonanza era mid-1800s–1920s; declined from clear-cutting
4. Oil — Spindletop Field first major wells; Railroad Commission of Texas has regulatory jurisdiction; Texas = 40% of U.S. oil production

Modern: Services = fastest-growing but lowest wages. USMCA replaced NAFTA 2020.

cattle cotton timber oil Spindletop Railroad Commission
21st Century Policy Challenges
Poverty: 1 in 4 children; 18.5% below poverty line; highest uninsured %; refused Medicaid expansion.
Education: Ranks 38th in high school graduation rate; 10%+ of teachers quit each year.
Water: Texas Water Development Board — demand will rise 22% by 2060.
Environment: Texas leads nation in many types of pollution; challenges federal EPA.
Immigration: Undocumented immigrants cost local govts ~$1.44B.
Chapter 2 — Federalism & the Texas Constitution
Powers, Interstate Relations & Texas Constitution of 1876
▼
Distribution of Powers
Delegated (enumerated/expressed) powers: Article I, Section 8 — specifically listed (coin money, declare war, regulate interstate commerce).
Implied powers: Necessary and Proper (elastic) Clause — allows Congress to expand authority.
Commerce Clause: Article I, Section 8 — regulation of interstate commerce; major tool for expanding federal power.
Reserved powers (10th Amendment): Powers not delegated to the federal government belong to states — police power, taxing power, proprietary power, eminent domain.

delegated = Art I Sec 8 implied = elastic clause Commerce Clause reserved = 10th Amend
Supremacy Clause & State Limitations
Supremacy Clause (Article VI): The Constitution and laws made under its authority are the supreme law of the land; all state officials are bound by it.
Limitations on states (Article I, Section 10): Cannot enter foreign treaties; cannot secede (Texas v. White); cannot coin money; 14th Amendment: cannot deny due process or equal protection.

Supremacy Clause = Art VI Art I Sec 10 Texas v. White = no secession 14th Amendment
Guarantees to States & Interstate Relations
Guarantees: Protection from invasion/domestic violence; cannot be combined or divided without consent; 2 senators per state; at least 1 House member.
Privileges and Immunities (Article IV): Citizens of each state entitled to same rights in all other states.
Full Faith and Credit (Article IV): Civil government actions of one state must be recognized in all states. Does NOT apply to criminal cases (extradition).
11th Amendment — Sovereign Immunity: States generally cannot be sued in federal court.

Privileges & Immunities = Art IV Full Faith & Credit 11th Amend = sovereign immunity
Federal-State Relations & Evolution
Grants-in-aid: Federal money with conditions attached — primary 20th-century tool for extending federal jurisdiction.
Devolution: Shifting of expenditures and responsibilities from federal to state governments (trend since 1980s/Reagan). Texas supports this; has filed 60+ lawsuits against the federal government since 2008 asserting 10th Amendment rights.
Block grants: Fixed sums of money on automatic formula — give states more flexibility than categorical grants.

grants-in-aid devolution (since 1980s) block grants
Texas Constitution of 1876 — Key Facts
Texas's 7th and current constitution. Drafted in reaction to Reconstruction/Davis administration.
Composition: 75 Democrats, 15 Republicans; 40%+ Texas Grange members. Slogan: "retrenchment and reform."
Creates a weak governor (one of the weakest gubernatorial offices in the nation). ~92,000 words; amended 500+ times. Less than 10% voter turnout in amendment elections.

Amendment process: Joint resolution passed by 2/3 vote in each legislative chamber → published → simple majority of voters in regular or special election → governor proclaims.

Texas's 7 constitutions: 1827 (Coahuila y Tejas) → 1836 (Republic) → 1845 (Statehood) → 1861 (Secession) → 1866 (Reconstruction) → 1869 → 1876 (current)
Texas annexed 1845. Unique right: may divide into up to five states with consent of legislature and Congress.

7 constitutions 1876 = current weak governor 2/3 each chamber + simple majority voters annexed 1845
